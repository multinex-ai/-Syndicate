# Documentary structures

Pick the structure that matches the question. Put its name in `outline.structure`.

## 1. Question-led investigation
Fits: "Why did X happen?", "Who is responsible?", anything with a puzzle.
Beats:
1. **The hook.** A concrete strange fact or image. State the question.
2. **The obvious answer.** What most people assume, and the evidence for it.
3. **The crack.** One piece of evidence that does not fit.
4. **Following the crack.** Two or three chapters, each a new source or angle that pulls further from the obvious answer.
5. **The midpoint turn.** The real mechanism comes into view (often a system, an incentive, a slow process).
6. **The strongest objection.** Argue against your own answer.
7. **The answer, and what it costs to know it.** Return to the hook image.

## 2. Chronological with a frame
Fits: histories, disasters, missions, biographies.
Beats:
1. **Cold open at the most dramatic moment.** Then cut back: "To understand how we got here, go back to..."
2. **The world before.** What was normal, and who believed what.
3. **The inciting change.**
4. **Escalation in dated steps.** Each chapter one period. Put the date on the chapter card.
5. **The climax.** Catch up with the cold open.
6. **Aftermath.** What changed, what did not, what it means now.
Rule: never compress dates to sound tidy. If two events were eleven years apart, say so.

## 3. Problem, cause, consequence, response
Fits: explainers on environment, health, infrastructure, economics.
Beats:
1. **Show the problem through one place or person.**
2. **Scale it.** One number with a comparison the viewer can feel ("an area the size of Wales", not "20,000 km²" alone).
3. **Causes**, ordered from the most visible to the deepest.
4. **Consequences**, ordered by who pays and when.
5. **Responses that were tried.** What worked, what failed and why.
6. **The open question.** What would have to be true for it to get better.

## 4. The journey (place or process)
Fits: following a river, a supply chain, a signal, a migration, a product.
Beats: one chapter per stage of the journey, each with one surprise. The final chapter returns to the start and shows it differently. Transitions between chapters can be literal moves (pan in the direction of travel, `slideleft` or `smoothleft`).

## 5. Then and now (contrast)
Fits: archive footage plus present-day footage of the same place or practice.
Beats alternate between then and now, with each pair making one point. Use the same framing or motion in each pair so the cut lands. Close on the widest "now" shot.

## 6. Portrait
Fits: one person, crew, institution or machine.
Beats: what they are known for; what they wanted; the obstacle; the method; the cost; the legacy, contested if it is contested. Use only on-record words and facts from sources.

## 7. Essay
Fits: an idea rather than an event ("why we measure things").
Beats: an image, a claim, three examples that each test the claim from a different side, a complication, a revised claim. The essay is the one structure where the narrator's own analysis carries the film, so mark those chapters `editorial analysis`.

## Chapter-level checks
- Does each chapter end with a turn, and does the next chapter start from it?
- Is there a reason to keep watching at every chapter card? Phrase chapter titles as small promises ("The number nobody checked"), not labels ("Background").
- Are the most important claims carried by the strongest sources and the best footage?
